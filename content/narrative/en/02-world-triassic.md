The Triassic is the emotional centre of the Dolomites story, and the globe cannot show it properly. What the globe can show is where the region was. Continents and the marker come from the PALEOMAP model of Scotese & Wright 2018, the same reconstruction behind the PaleoDEM that colours the land and the sea; the model draws plate boundaries only for the last 100 million years, so none appear here [@scotese-2018]. The current international time scale, ICS chart 2026/06, places the base of the Middle Triassic at 247.0 million years [@ics-2026].

Adria drifts across the northern tropics, but exactly where is contested. For 240 million years ago the displayed model puts the Dolomites at about 7 degrees north [@scotese-2018]. Other global models, computed for this site from their published rotation files, put the same point between about 1 degree north (Müller et al. 2019) and about 12 degrees north (Merdith et al. 2021) [@muller-2019; @merdith-2021]. Paleomagnetic measurements on the Seceda section, in the Dolomites themselves, give about 16 to 18 degrees north [@muttoni-1997]. The models use different reference frames, and paleomagnetic latitudes carry their own uncertainty.

Above the moving marker, a warm shallow arm of Tethys spreads out. On this shelf carbonate platforms grow into towers, deep basins settle black hemipelagic muds, and volcanoes cut briefly through. None of this is resolvable on the globe: a single grid cell of the high-resolution PaleoDEM is roughly the size of the Sella group. The reefs belong to the next chapter, which switches to the terrain [@bosellini-2003].
