The last frame is the one you started on, but with the change made visible. The modern glacier outlines are RGI 7.0 [@rgi-7]. The Little Ice Age extent behind them is the Reinthaler and Paul 2025 reconstruction, an open dataset that covers the whole Alps in fourteen sub-regions, explicitly including the Dolomites [@reinthaler-paul-2025].

Across the entire European Alps, glacier area has fallen from about 4244 square kilometres at the Little Ice Age peak around 1850 to about 1806 square kilometres in 2015, a loss of 57 per cent. Volume has fallen from about 280 to about 100 cubic kilometres, a loss of 64 per cent. Mean surface lowering is 43.7 metres; at least 1938 glaciers have disappeared entirely [@reinthaler-paul-2025].

The Dolomites' own numbers are worse. A recent Cryosphere synthesis puts local glacier area at 4.11 square kilometres in the modern baseline and 1.81 square kilometres today - a loss of 56 per cent - with about a third of that loss falling in the 13 years between 2010 and 2023, and a mean mass balance of minus 0.64 metres water equivalent per year [@securo-2025].

The rock, meanwhile, is still rising: about 1.0 to 1.5 millimetres a year across the Dolomites, measured by GNSS [@sanchez-2018]. The story does not end.
