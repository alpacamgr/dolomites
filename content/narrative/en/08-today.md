The last frame is the one you started on, but with the change made visible. The modern glacier outlines are RGI 7.0 [@rgi-7]. The Little Ice Age extent behind them is the Reinthaler and Paul 2025 reconstruction, an open dataset that covers the whole Alps in fourteen sub-regions and explicitly includes the Dolomites [@reinthaler-paul-2025].

Across the whole European Alps, glacier area has fallen by more than half since the Little Ice Age peak around 1850, and glacier volume by almost two thirds. The mean surface lowering is over forty metres; at least 1938 glaciers have vanished entirely [@reinthaler-paul-2025].

The Dolomites' own numbers are worse. A recent Cryosphere synthesis finds local glacier area more than halved since the modern baseline, about a third of that loss falling in the thirteen years between 2010 and 2023 [@securo-2025].

The rock, meanwhile, is still rising: about one to one and a half millimetres a year across the Dolomites, measured by GNSS [@sanchez-2018]. The story does not end.
