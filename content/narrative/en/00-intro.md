These are the Dolomites as they stand today. Scroll up, and time runs backward, three hundred million years of it. The story alternates between two stages. A globe carries the plate-tectonic chapters, because plate motion only makes sense at that scale. This same terrain, coloured by the age of the rock, carries the local chapters: the Triassic reefs, the Alpine collision, the ice ages, the present.

Everything on the screen carries one of four honesty labels. Observed is measured today. Modeled comes from a published numerical model. Interpreted is a researcher's map drawn from field evidence. Illustrative is drawing. If a fact has no source, it does not appear.

The relief you see was measured, not painted. Inside Italy it comes from the ten-metre elevation model TINITALY, published by INGV [@tarquini-2023]; across the border, from the thirty-metre global Copernicus DEM [@copernicus-dem]. The colours on the rock come from open geological maps: South Tyrol [@bolzano-geologia] and Trentino [@pat-geologia] at fine scale, the Veneto lithology map for the Belluno side [@veneto-litologia], and, where those stop, swisstopo's GeoCover for Switzerland [@swisstopo-geocover], the ISPRA 1:100,000 map surveyed between 1880 and 1980 for the rest of Italy [@ispra-geologia-100k], and the GeoSphere Austria 1:500,000 map for Austria [@geosphere-ge-500k]. Each unit is coloured by its age on the international time scale. Light grey marks rock that is mapped but has no reliable age; hatching marks areas with no open map.
