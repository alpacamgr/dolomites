The narrow ocean closes. Adria pushes north into Europe, oceanic crust is consumed at Alpine subduction zones, and a mountain belt is born. The globe follows this in the PALEOMAP model of Scotese & Wright 2018 [@scotese-2018]. For the Alpine kinematics, the Müller et al. 2019 model and the regional Le Breton et al. 2021 update are internally consistent because the two share a rotation-file lineage [@muller-2019; @lebreton-2021].

At this scale the Dolomites are a small point on Adria. What Adria itself is has been argued about since Argand in the 1920s. In one view Adria is a promontory of Africa, never truly separate. In the other it is an independent microplate, divided from Africa by an intervening oceanic domain. Jolivet's 2023 review notes that paleomagnetic error bars alone cover several hundred kilometres and cannot on their own settle the question; ophiolite evidence from Cyprus and southern Turkey is used to argue for the oceanic case [@jolivet-2023].

Whichever version wins, the push is not over. GNSS measurements in the ALPS2017 solution show that the Southern Eastern Alps, and the Dolomites with them, are still rising at about one to one and a half millimetres a year [@sanchez-2018].
