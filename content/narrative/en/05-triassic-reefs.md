The Middle Triassic reefs are not a reconstruction on this screen. They are the mountains themselves. In the Anisian and Ladinian, isolated carbonate platforms - Schlern/Sciliar, Latemar, Sella, Marmolada, Rosengarten/Catinaccio, Civetta and others - grew on the shallow Tethyan shelf, separated by deep basins that filled with dark hemipelagic muds and volcaniclastic turbidites [@bosellini-2003]. Because the Dolomites were only lightly deformed later, the platform-and-basin geometry is preserved essentially intact. Colouring the modern terrain by rock unit literally draws the reefs on the peaks that carry their names.

The Latemar is the classic case, listed by the IUGS as a global geoheritage site [@iugs-latemar]. Reconstructions of the Schlern/Sciliar platform give it about 800 metres of preserved thickness, taken as a proxy for the maximum platform-to-basin relief in the western Dolomites [@marangon-2021].

How long the platforms took to build is contested. Cyclostratigraphy of Milankovitch precession bands gives the Latemar at least nine million years; U-Pb ages on interbedded tuffs allow only one to two million. The Latemar controversy is unresolved [@iugs-latemar]. High-precision zircon U-Pb ages show that the main interval of Middle Triassic magmatism in the Southern Alps lasted at least 5.07 ± 0.06 million years: silicic eruptions between 242.653 ± 0.036 and 238.646 ± 0.037 Ma, then an eruptive interval of less than 1 million years of voluminous basaltic lava flows. Coeval mafic to intermediate intrusions, dated at 238.190 ± 0.055 to 238.075 ± 0.087 Ma, may represent feeder and subvolcanic complexes of those flows, and the youngest products are silicic tuffs dated at 237.680 ± 0.047 and 237.579 ± 0.042 Ma [@storck-2019].
