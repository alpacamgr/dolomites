Pangea breaks, and a narrow ocean opens between Europe and Adria: the Alpine Tethys, with its western arm the Piemont-Liguria basin. The globe shows this age in the PALEOMAP model of Scotese & Wright 2018, which has plate boundaries only for the last 100 million years; they appear only in the later part of this chapter [@scotese-2018]. The Alpine detail comes from a separate regional reconstruction. Le Breton et al. 2021, layered onto the Müller et al. 2019 backbone, treat Adria as two plates - a northern Ivrea piece and a southern Apulian piece - and reconstruct the opening of the Piemont-Liguria basin in four stages between 200 and 83 million years ago [@lebreton-2021; @muller-2019].

The mature Piemont-Liguria ocean is not the sprawling Tethys of school atlases. In the Le Breton model it holds at most about 250 kilometres of true oceanic crust between Adria and Europe [@lebreton-2021]. Other reconstructions draw it wider or narrower, and paleomagnetic uncertainty covers several hundred kilometres either way.

Under the ocean, the Dolomites are the passive margin: they drown. The Bosellini, Gianolla and Stefani synthesis records the full Permian-to-Cretaceous succession of the region as a Tethyan passive margin sequence, with the Middle Jurassic marking the oceanic break-up [@bosellini-2003]. White Biancone chalks and red Rosso Ammonitico limestones settle in the deep water; there are no more reefs.
