For the last chapter of the story, ice takes over the terrain. Seguinot and colleagues in 2018 ran the Parallel Ice Sheet Model across the entire Alpine arc through the full last glacial cycle, from 120 thousand years ago to today, on a one-kilometre grid. Their continuous-variable Zenodo release holds ice thickness, velocity and surface elevation as thousand-year snapshots that this site interpolates as you scroll [@seguinot-2018]. The Last Glacial Maximum outline drawn behind the modelled ice comes from the open GLACIMONTIS database [@flantua-2026].

In the model, the ice above Bolzano is thickest around twenty-four thousand years ago, at about 2150 metres. That figure is model output for one grid cell, not a measurement [@seguinot-2018].

Deglaciation was quick where it began. Cosmogenic exposure ages in Alta Badia record the collapse of the high-valley glaciers from about seventeen thousand years ago, with later Lateglacial re-advances [@rossato-2025]. When the ice left, the unbuttressed valley walls failed. The Marocche di Dro in the Sarca valley are among the largest Holocene rockslides in the Alps: 36Cl dates place the Marocca Principale about 5300 years ago and the Kas event about 1080 years ago, close to human historical memory [@ivyochs-2017].
