For the last chapter of Earth history, the ice model comes forward. Seguinot and colleagues in 2018 ran the Parallel Ice Sheet Model over the entire Alpine arc for the full last glacial cycle, from 120 thousand years ago to today, at a one-kilometre horizontal grid; the continuous-variable Zenodo release stores ice thickness, velocity and surface elevation as 1000-year snapshots that this site interpolates as you scroll [@seguinot-2018]. The Last Glacial Maximum extent outline is taken from the open GLACIMONTIS geodatabase [@flantua-2026].

In the model, the ice above Bolzano is thickest around 24 thousand years ago, at about 2150 metres; that figure is model output for one grid cell, not a measurement [@seguinot-2018].

Deglaciation was quick where it began. Cosmogenic exposure ages in Alta Badia record the collapse of the high-valley glaciers from about 17 thousand years ago, with later Lateglacial re-advances [@rossato-2025]. When the ice left, the unbuttressed valley walls failed. The Marocche di Dro in the Sarca valley are among the largest Holocene rockslides in the Alps: 36Cl dates place the Marocca Principale at 5300 ± 860 years ago, and the Kas event at 1080 ± 160 years, close to human historical memory [@ivyochs-2017].
