# Geological history of the Dolomites — sourced chronology

Working draft. Compiled 2026-09-06. Sources at end.

Time axis: International Chronostratigraphic Chart, ICS **v2026/06** (released June 2026, per ICS news post — source 1c [verified 2026-09-06]). The 2026/06 revision updated three stage-base ages relevant to this file: **base Anisian 247.0 Ma** (was 246.7 Ma), **base Olenekian 250.8 Ma** (was 249.9 Ma), and **base Wuchiapingian 259.857 ± 0.084 Ma** (was 259.51 ± 0.21 Ma; also the base of the Lopingian). Next scheduled update 2026/12. Numeric ages below follow ICS 2026/06 where a boundary age is stated; primary-source age estimates are quoted as published.

Region: Dolomites (Southern Alps, provinces of Bolzano/South Tyrol, Trento, Belluno; Italy). The whole succession sits on the **Adria** microplate, which has moved in close conjunction with Africa at least since Permian times (source 12).

Region-wide picture (source 2, Bosellini, Gianolla & Stefani 2003): the exposed Dolomites are a piece of Southern Alps thick-skinned Alpine deformation of a Tethyan passive margin, with a Permo-Jurassic tectono-magmatic evolution (Permian magmatism, Middle Triassic rapid subsidence + volcanism, Jurassic oceanic break-up) overprinted by Alpine shortening (Eocene, and stronger Neogene S-vergent thrusting + strike-slip). Sedimentary succession = Middle Permian to Cretaceous; Triassic carbonates dominate.

---

## 1. Variscan basement (pre-Permian; Carboniferous back to Neoproterozoic protoliths)

- **Setting**: pre-Alpine metamorphic basement of the Southalpine domain, assembled and metamorphosed during the Variscan orogeny (Carboniferous). Represents the eastern extension of the Moldanubian domain (source 20).
- **Rocks**: chiefly low-grade metasediments — the **Brixen/Bressanone Quartzphyllite** — with peak conditions ~0.5–0.65 GPa, 450–550 °C reached near Brixen; grades decrease southward (source 20).
- **Exposure today**: valleys N and W of the sedimentary Dolomites cover; base of the sedimentary pile is best seen in the lower Val Pusteria / Val d'Isarco.
- **What it means for the story**: this is the "floor" the entire sedimentary Dolomites was later laid down on. It is not itself Dolomite scenery.

## 2. Early–Middle Permian — Athesian Volcanic Group ("Bolzano quartz porphyry")

- **Age**: activity spans ~285–274 Ma per the Marocchi et al. 2008 abstract (source 21) [verified 2026-09-12; corrected from ~284–274 and 279–274 Ma]; broader window 290–274 Ma cited elsewhere.
- **Setting**: post-Variscan extensional collapse; giant silicic (rhyodacite → rhyolite) caldera complex — the "Bolzano/Bozen supervolcano". Ignimbrites are the dominant preserved units; largest is the rhyodacitic Gargazzone Ignimbrite; overall compositional trend rhyodacitic–andesitic to rhyolitic after that caldera collapse (source 21). Ora Ignimbrite has been described as a supereruption-scale magma system (source 21a).
- **Localities**: Adige valley Merano–Bolzano, W Dolomites plateaus (Bolzano, Nova Ponente, Val di Cembra, Val d'Ega).
- **In the section**: unconformably overlies the Variscan basement; directly underlies the Val Gardena Sandstone.

## 3. Late Permian — Val Gardena / Gröden Sandstone

- **Age**: Wuchiapingian (early–middle Lopingian), late Permian (source 3, source 15).
- **Environment**: fully **terrestrial**. Red beds — an asymmetric large-scale fining-upward cycle of desert plain, alluvial and playa deposits under a semi-arid climate. Eroded material transported by short-lived rivers onto a desert plain (source 3).
- **Fossils**: tetrapod footprints; classic locality **Bletterbach gorge** near Aldein/Redagno (source 3, 15).
- **Progression**: interfingers upward with, and is overlain by, the Bellerophon Fm as the sea encroaches from the E (Palaeotethys).

## 4. Late Permian — Bellerophon Formation

- **Age**: Wuchiapingian–Changhsingian; uppermost beds Changhsingian (source 15).
- **Environment**: coastal **sabkha to shallow shelf** in a hot evaporative climate. Sulphate evaporites, marly dolostones, marlstones and neritic limestones. Rich in the gastropod *Bellerophon*, dasyclad algae, small foraminifera, algal mats, gypsum (source 15).
- **Localities**: Bletterbach, Butterloch, Sass de Putia.
- **Notes**: base of the marine Mesozoic record here. Its evaporites later mobilised as **diapirs** along N70°E flower structures in Middle Triassic transpressional tectonics (source 6, Doglioni 1987). Its top holds the Permian–Triassic mass-extinction level.

## 5. Early Triassic — Werfen Formation

- **Age**: Induan (Griesbachian) to Olenekian (Spathian); Early Triassic (source 16).
- **Environment**: mixed carbonate–siliciclastic **homoclinal ramp** on the western Palaeotethyan shelf at low northerly latitude (source 16). Multiple members recording repeated shallow subtidal to peritidal cycles.
- **Fossils**: bivalves and gastropods; a reference section for the biotic recovery after the end-Permian extinction — recovery slow, with elevated extinction rates again in the Dienerian and around the Smithian/Spathian boundary (source 16).
- **Localities**: widely exposed at the base of Dolomites cliffs (e.g. Val Badia, Ampezzo area, San Cassiano, Piz da Peres).

## 6. Middle Triassic — carbonate ramp onset (Lower Serla, Contrin) and rifting

- **Age**: Anisian (~247.0–241.5 Ma; Anisian–Ladinian boundary calibrated at **241.464 ± 0.064 Ma** by U–Pb on Buchenstein tuffs, source 22 Storck et al. 2018).
- **Setting**: broad, low-relief carbonate ramp — the **Contrin Formation** — sits on top of the Werfen and covers most of the W Dolomites during the late Anisian (source 23). Extensional faulting starts to break the ramp into structural highs (buildups) and lows (anoxic basins, e.g. Moena Fm).
- **Localities**: Contrin type area, Costabella; widely present as a marker between Werfen and the overlying Sciliar / Latemar platforms.

## 7. Anisian–Ladinian — high-relief carbonate platforms and Buchenstein/Livinallongo basins

This is the classic Dolomites platform-and-basin system, the reason the geology is famous.

- **Age**: latest Anisian – Ladinian (~242–237 Ma). Anisian–Ladinian boundary 241.464 ± 0.064 Ma (source 22).
- **Setting**: extensional/transtensional tectonics fragments the shelf into isolated **high-relief carbonate platforms** (steep clinoform slopes, ~30° initial dip; source 24 IUGS Latemar) separated by **deep basins** filled with hemipelagic and volcaniclastic deposits.
  - Platforms: **Sciliar / Schlern Dolomite** (~800 m thick, latest Anisian–early Ladinian, source 4a), **Latemar** (isolated, late Anisian; source 24), Marmolada, Rosengarten/Catinaccio, Sella, Civetta, Pale di San Martino.
  - Basins: **Livinallongo / Buchenstein Fm** (Ladinian, hemipelagic pyritic limestones with tuff layers "Pietra Verde"), **Wengen Fm** (volcaniclastic turbidites), Zoppè, Fernazza.
- **Platform-to-basin relief**: "basin many hundreds of meters deep" at the base of Latemar slopes (source 24). Sciliar/Schlern reached ~800 m platform thickness (source 4a). Later Carnian progradation records a platform-to-basin relief of "at least 100 m" in the final stage (source 4a — note: this is the last stage of Carnian progradation, not the Ladinian maximum, which was several hundred m).
- **Localities**: Latemar (IUGS Geoheritage site), Schlern/Sciliar plateau, Sella, Pale di San Martino, Marmolada, Cinque Torri.

### Debate: Latemar duration
The Latemar cycles have been interpreted as **Milankovitch precession** cycles totalling **≥9 My** for the ~470 m succession (source 25 Preto et al., in Cyclostratigraphy volume). High-precision U–Pb zircon ages on interbedded tuffs are only compatible with a duration of **~1–2 My**, closer to biostratigraphic estimates. The "Latemar controversy" (cyclostrat vs radiometric) is unresolved in a fully satisfying way — either not all cycles are Milankovitch, or the geochronology is missing something, or a shorter (sub-Milankovitch) forcing period applies. Zühlke 2003 (source 25a) explicitly argues for sub-Milankovitch forcing.

## 8. Middle Triassic (Ladinian) magmatism — Predazzo, Monzoni

- **Age**: a short-lived intrusive pulse within a longer magmatic episode of at least 5.07 ± 0.06 My (Storck et al. 2019 abstract, source 5) [verified 2026-09-12; corrected from "extremely short-lived magmatic pulse"]. High-precision U–Pb zircon on mafic to intermediate intrusions of the Monzoni–Predazzo complex: **238.190 ± 0.050 to 238.075 ± 0.087 Ma**; granitic phases at Predazzo **237.3 ± 1.0 Ma**; late-Ladinian to early-Carnian tuffs **237.680 ± 0.047 and 237.579 ± 0.042 Ma**. Total duration constrained to **< ~0.7 My** (source 5, Storck et al. 2019, J. Geol. Soc.).
- **Products**: Predazzo Intrusive Complex (~4.5 km³ gabbro → syenite → syenogranite), ~6 km³ basaltic to latitic volcanics, extensive dyke swarm. ENE–WSW elongation controlled by Ladinian strike-slip faults.
- **Signature**: alkaline / shoshonitic geochemistry — commonly interpreted as reflecting a **subduction-modified sub-continental lithospheric mantle** inherited from Variscan subduction (source 20a).

### Debate: cause of Ladinian magmatism
Well-established: it happened, it was fast (<1 My), and the mantle source carries a Variscan subduction "fingerprint" (source 20a). Debated: whether the trigger was (a) transtensional lithospheric thinning in a strike-slip corridor, (b) delamination / slab break-off of remnant Variscan slab, or (c) far-field extension linked to opening of Neotethys. Bosellini et al. 2003 (source 2) frame it as a Middle Triassic tectono-magmatic event within an actively rifting margin.

## 9. Late Ladinian – early Carnian — Cassian Dolomite and San Cassiano Fm

- **Age**: latest Ladinian – Carnian (~237–232 Ma).
- **Setting**: renewed carbonate platform progradation ("Cassian Dolomite") builds out over volcanic and basinal deposits; laterally the deep basins fill with the marly, fossil-rich **San Cassiano Formation** (Cipit boulders — allochthonous reefal blocks in basinal marls with world-class preservation of ammonoids, gastropods, bivalves, echinoderms, sponges, corals; source 17).
- **Localities**: Alpe di Specie, Misurina, Val Badia around San Cassiano, Cortina d'Ampezzo surroundings (Museo Zardini).

## 10. Carnian — Carnian Pluvial Event and demise of high-relief platforms

- **Age**: late early Carnian (Julian 2, ~234 Ma).
- **Event**: a short (~1 My) humid climate perturbation — the **Carnian Pluvial Event** — coincides with the crisis and termination of the high-relief Ladinian–early-Carnian carbonate platforms (source 26 Stefani, Furin & Gianolla 2010; also Gattolin et al. Termination of Carbonate Slope Progradation).
- **Result**: platforms are drowned or buried, siliciclastic and marly-carbonate input increases across the region.

## 11. Late Carnian — Travenanzes / Raibl Group

- **Age**: late Carnian (~232–228 Ma).
- **Environment**: return to arid climate. Mixed siliciclastic–carbonate coastal and continental system — **alluvial plain / sabkha / ephemeral lake / lagoon** — with evaporites, red and green shales, aphanitic dolostones (source 27). Sediment from highlands to the south, opening northeast to Tethys.
- **Localities**: Val Travenanzes (type), Ampezzo area, Fanes, Sella base.

## 12. Norian–Rhaetian — Dolomia Principale / Hauptdolomit

- **Age**: Norian to earliest Rhaetian (~228–201 Ma).
- **Environment**: a huge, laterally continuous peritidal **carbonate tidal-flat** to shallow subtidal system covering the whole Adriatic epicontinental shelf. Unusually stable, hot, semi-arid climate (source 28). Thickness up to **~2 km** in the Southern Alps (source 28).
- **Rock**: massive dolomite, well-bedded in cycles — the socle of most of the Dolomiti Bellunesi and much of the "big walls".
- **Localities**: Pale di San Martino, Marmolada base, Antelao, Sorapiss, Pelmo, Sella (upper part), Civetta, Schiara, most of the National Park Dolomiti Bellunesi.

## 13. Latest Triassic – Early Jurassic — Calcari Grigi Group and Trento Platform onset

- **Age**: Hettangian–Pliensbachian (~201–184 Ma).
- **Environment**: rifting-related differentiation. In the Dolomites area, a shallow-water peritidal to subtidal **carbonate platform** — the **Calcari Grigi Group** — develops, part of the wider ~100-km-wide **Trento Platform** horst (source 29).
- **Fossils**: peritidal facies host dinosaur footprints (Mt Pelmo top, Sarca Valley).
- **Localities**: Rotzo, Loppio; in the Dolomites the Calcari Grigi caps some southern peaks.

## 14. Pliensbachian – Toarcian — drowning, Rosso Ammonitico Inferiore, Tenno Fm

- **Age**: ~183 Ma (Pliensbachian–Toarcian transition) onwards.
- **Event**: Trento Platform enters a carbonate production crisis; Tenno Fm (chert-bearing marly limestones) superimposes on Calcari Grigi (source 29). Continued rifting subsides the platform; drowning finalises in the Middle Jurassic.

## 15. Middle–Late Jurassic — Rosso Ammonitico Veronese

- **Age**: upper Bajocian to upper Tithonian (~170–145 Ma); three members RAI (upper Bajocian–upper Callovian), RAM (upper Callovian–middle Oxfordian), RAS (middle Oxfordian–upper Tithonian) (source 30).
- **Environment**: **pelagic, condensed, red nodular limestone** on the drowned Trento Plateau, with abundant ammonites; deposited on a submerged high flanked by the Lombardia Basin (W) and Belluno Basin (E) (source 30, 31).
- **In the Belluno Basin**: coeval deeper-water facies — Soverzene, Igne, Vajont Limestone (large submarine landslides), Fonzaso Fm (chert-bearing) — recorded in Dolomiti Bellunesi (source 8, dolomitipark.it).
- **Localities today**: southern edge of the Dolomites — Vallarsa, Puez plateau, etc.

## 16. Early Cretaceous — Biancone / Maiolica

- **Age**: latest Jurassic (Tithonian) to Aptian; the Biancone (local name for the Tethyan **Maiolica**) is Upper Hauterivian–Barremian in the classic definition (source 31).
- **Environment**: **pelagic to hemipelagic** nannofossil limestone, ~90 % CaCO₃, white/ivory with chert nodules. Records a fully open Tethyan ocean setting.
- **Localities**: Puez, Sass de Stria, southern Dolomites, Belluno Basin.

## 17. Late Cretaceous – earliest Paleogene — Scaglia Rossa

- **Age**: Upper Cretaceous – lower Eocene (source 32).
- **Environment**: deep-marine marly limestones with clay input from newly emergent lands = **first Alpine orogenic signal** in the sediments; brick-red to grey-rose. Turbidites and syn-sedimentary submarine landslides (Sasso Rosso in N Brenta).
- **Localities**: Brenta, Belluno Basin, Talvena, Mt. Brendol.

## 18. Paleogene — Belluno Flysch and early Alpine deformation

- **Age**: early to middle Eocene.
- **Setting**: turbiditic **flysch** up to ~1000 m thick fills a **foredeep** of the west-vergent Dinaric thrust system; sedimentary response to the Mesoalpine (Eocene) compressional phase — first significant Alpine shortening reaches the Dolomites area (source 33, 34).
- **Sedimentology**: overlies Scaglia Rossa transitionally in Valbelluna; classic amber-bearing horizons (Pont, Belluno).

## 19. Oligocene–Miocene — main Alpine deformation of the Dolomites (Southalpine thrust belt)

- **Setting**: Adria indents into the Alps. The Dolomites become the internal part of a **south-vergent thin-to-thick-skinned thrust belt**, bounded:
  - N by the **Insubric/Periadriatic Lineament** (dextral transpression, late Oligocene–early Miocene) (source 35).
  - W by the **Giudicarie fault system** — an offset of the Insubric line with apparent ~70 km left-lateral displacement; late Miocene sinistral transpressive reactivation of an older late-Oligocene reverse fault; total ~40–47 km sinistral offset (source 35).
  - S by the **Valsugana thrust system** — main Neogene, S-directed. Hanging-wall uplift ~4 km in the late Miocene, driving regional uplift of **3–5 km** (source 7). Low-temperature thermochronology (AFT, AHe) narrows the main Valsugana shortening / cooling phase in the Dolomites to **~17–9 Ma** (source 38 Zattin et al. 2006, cited via review — search summary).
- **Present structure**: a large **synclinorium** cut internally by NE–SW-striking, SE-verging thrusts; older Middle-Triassic transpressional flower structures along N70°E, involving Bellerophon-evaporite diapirs, are still visible (source 6, Doglioni 1987).

### Debate: how much Alpine deformation did the Dolomites experience, and when did the Dolomites become emergent land?
- Well-established: much of the present relief is Neogene, driven by Valsugana-system shortening in the Miocene and later (source 7).
- Debated / not settled by sources I fetched: exact timing at which the Dolomites first emerged permanently above sea level. The sedimentary record shows deep-marine deposition into the Paleogene (Belluno Flysch and Scaglia Rossa); permanent emergence and mountain-scale relief seem to date from Miocene Southalpine shortening, but a single crisp emergence age is not stated in the primary sources fetched. **No source found** in my search giving a specific Ma emergence age.
- Compared to the Central/Western Alps, the Dolomites are widely described as only lightly metamorphosed and moderately deformed — the sedimentary architecture is preserved essentially intact, which is what makes the Ladinian platform-basin geometries visible in outcrop.

## 20. Quaternary — glaciation, landslides, modern landscape

- **LGM ice cover**: at ~20 ka, glaciers filled all major Dolomites valleys, thicknesses **>1500 m, up to ~2000 m in the Bolzano area** (source 8, dolomitiunesco / source 36 EGQSJ Valsugana glacier network).
- **Deglaciation**: valley glaciers collapse in the Lateglacial, dated by cosmogenic exposure ages in high Dolomites valleys (source 37).
- **Post-glacial mass movements**:
  - **Marocche di Dro** (Sarca valley, Trentino) — Marocca Principale rock avalanche **5300 ± 860 yr BP** (³⁶Cl); Kas collapse **1080 ± 160 yr BP** (³⁶Cl); Kas trigger tentatively linked to the 1046 CE Middle Adige Valley earthquake (source 9 Ivy-Ochs et al. 2017 QSR).
  - Slide clusters around Cortina d'Ampezzo (Antelao, Sorapiss debris fans) after LGM.
- **Modern glaciers**: shrinking remnants (Marmolada, Adamello‑Presanella outside the Dolomites s.s.).

### Q2. Well-established vs debated
Well-established:
- Sedimentary succession Middle Permian → Cretaceous with dominant Triassic carbonates; formation sequence and gross ages agreed across sources 2, 8, 13, 15–17, 27–34.
- Adria has moved with Africa since at least the Permian; Southern Alps were part of the parautochthonous Adria margin (source 12).
- Middle Triassic magmatism lasted at least 5.07 ± 0.06 My: silicic volcanism 242.653–238.646 Ma, then under 1 My of basaltic flows; intrusions dated 238.190–238.075 Ma may represent their feeders (Storck et al. 2019 abstract, source 5) [verified 2026-09-12; corrected from "<~0.7 My"].
- Neogene S-vergent Southalpine shortening (Valsugana system) is the main driver of present relief (source 7).
- LGM valley-glacier thicknesses of >1500 m in the Dolomites (sources 8, 36).

Debated / open:
- **Cause of Ladinian magmatism** — transtension vs. delamination vs. inherited Variscan subduction signal vs. far-field back-arc effect. Geochemistry supports a subduction-modified mantle source (source 20a); tectonic trigger not fully settled.
- **Duration of the Latemar** and by extension the "Latemar controversy" — cyclostratigraphic ≥9 My vs. radiometric ~1–2 My (sources 25, 25a).
- **Exact timing of the Dolomites' permanent emergence above sea level** — not pinned to a single Ma value in the sources fetched; consensus is Miocene, from Valsugana-system uplift (source 7).
- **How much Alpine deformation the Dolomites experienced** — quantitative shortening estimates vary; qualitatively low relative to the Central Alps (Dolomites remain essentially unmetamorphosed).
- **Triassic paleolatitude of Adria (Dolomites)** — Muttoni et al. (Seceda section) place the Dolomites at **~16–18° N** (intertropical, N hemisphere) during the Middle Triassic (~238–242 Ma window of Buchenstein tuffs) — source 12b (search summary of Muttoni, Nicora, Brack, Kent). This is a specific paleolatitude number; a formal ±error bar was not captured in the search summary and should be re-verified from the primary PDF. Consensus is that Adria moved with Africa (source 12 Muttoni et al. APWP).
